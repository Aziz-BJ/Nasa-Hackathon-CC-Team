## The mismatch

The app was built for a global grid: any drawn shape sums the grid cells inside it (1° in the mock data). The real record is **per region**: mainland Italy, Sicily, Sardinia, California. That is by design, not a gap: the factors are fitted per region and do not transfer between regions (leave-one-region-out), so there is no harmonized value for a single 5 km cell or a custom shape inside a region.

So the app moves from "cells inside your shape" to "regions you pick". Everything else (calendar, trends, sensors, CSV, date bar, guide, design) stays and gets better data: real 90% ranges, the team's own anomaly definitions, and a proper month-to-date comparison.

## Outcome

Pierre can re-run the importer after any new pipeline run. `docs/DATA_CONTRACT.md` and `docs/write_app_data.py` are rewritten for this shape. The 10° tiles, overview layer and mock generator go; unit tests use a small region fixture instead.

**2. Choosing an area.** The Area panel lists the four regions plus "All of Italy" (the three Italian regions together). You can also:
- click a region on the globe;
- draw a circle or shape: it selects every region the shape touches;
- type coordinates: they select the region that contains the point.

Outside the four regions the app says so plainly ("The record covers mainland Italy, Sicily, Sardinia and California"). For combined regions the totals add exactly; the 90% range is the sum of the regions' ranges, which is wider than the true range, and the app says so.

**3. The globe.** The four regions are filled and the selected one is outlined. The fill shows how the chosen months compare with the same months in 2003–2025, using the five verdict classes (well below … well above) on a colour-blind-safe diverging ramp. A count ramp across the four regions would always paint California darkest, because it is 17 times larger than Sardinia. The bottom strip's legend follows.

**4. Numbers and units.** Every count becomes **fire cell-days (Aqua MODIS scale)**: a 5 km square with at least one detection in a day or night pass. One sentence explains this in the guide and in About. Values show with their 90% range, e.g. "October 2026: 50 fire cell-days (90% range 30–74)". Measured Aqua months (to June 2021) have no range, and the app says they are measured.

**5. Statistics match the method.** Baseline 2003–2025 with the target year left out, median, Δ% (n/a when the median is 0), robust z (median and MAD), percentile = share of baseline years at or below the value. A unit test checks the app against all 1,168 rows of `calendar_anomalies.csv`. The verdict thresholds stay at 10/25/75/90.

**6. The unfinished month.** November 2026 (1–13 Nov) is compared with 1–13 November of every past year from `mtd.json`, never with whole months: "1–13 Nov: 1.9 fire cell-days · past years' median for the same days: 2".

**7. Panels.**
- Calendar: real values. Jul–Sep 2002 are marked incomplete (days without any satellite), Aug–Nov 2026 provisional (NRT) and Nov 2026 partial. The detail line gives the value, the 90% range, the source (measured Aqua, converted from S-NPP, chained from NOAA-20, or mixed with days per source), Δ%, percentile and z.
- Trends: the same two charts, with the 90% band.
- Sensors: raw Aqua, S-NPP and NOAA-20 against the harmonized line, which shows the false jump a naive splice makes (Pierre's figure 2), and the region's factor table (S-NPP → Aqua by season and day/night).
- Layers: harmonized, raw Aqua MODIS, raw S-NPP, raw NOAA-20.
- About: method in plain words, validation (monthly error 1.54 → 0.33 against no correction, R² 0.98, 89% of true values inside the 90% range, no step at either sensor switch), the limits from METHOD §10, and credits.

**8. Dates.** The date bar runs Jul 2002 – Nov 2026, because the record starts with Aqua.

## Decisions after Andrea's files (15 Nov)

**Outlines.** Andrea's `region_outlines_all_four_regions.geojson` (the land outlines his `regions.py` uses: ISTAT regions for Italy, US Census TIGER 2010 for California), simplified for the web. Italian regions also own a 3 nautical-mile sea margin, said in About.

**The map: 5 km fire cells that add up to Pierre's totals.** Andrea's `cell_days.csv` has every fire cell-day per 5 km cell and satellite. For each day the app's importer takes the same satellite the record uses (Aqua to June 2021, S-NPP to 1 Nov 2026, NOAA-20 after, with the same missing-day fills), counts Aqua cell-days as they are and converts S-NPP and NOAA-20 cell-days with Pierre's factor for that region, season and day/night. Each region-month is then scaled so its cells add up exactly to Pierre's `H`, which carries the fire-size term the factor table leaves out.

Checks on the real files:
- the rebuilt measured months equal `H` in all 904 of 904;
- raw counts per satellite equal Pierre's in all 1,172 region-months;
- in converted months, `H` is 0.47 to 2.11 times the cells' unscaled sum (median 0.93; 90% of months between 0.67 and 1.35). This range was corrected after review: it first said 0.65–1.33, which is only the 5th–95th percentile range.

So:
- **Globe:** the four regions are outlined and tinted by the verdict for the latest chosen month; click one to select it. The selected area shows its 5 km fire cells, coloured by fire cell-days in the chosen months, with bins that fit those months.
- **Areas:** a region (or All of Italy) uses Pierre's numbers and 90% range. A drawn shape or typed coordinates inside a region again sums the cells inside it. That is an estimate (the region's conversion applied to part of it), with no 90% range, and the app labels it so. Raw sensor counts are exact for any shape.
